Evidence-backed suggestions for TONE.md, extracted from `activity-notes.txt` (Pass 1: 45 tone-mentioned activities) + the 12 non-beta activities (Pass 2) + a skim of structural notes for buried tone issues (Pass 3).

**16 candidate amendments**, grouped as: **11 gaps** (new rules), **3 buried** (expand existing), **2 cross-ref / scope-broadening**. Plus a separate **cross-reference list** and **dropped clusters** at the end.

Review per cluster; approve, reject, or rewrite. Only accepted ones will be rolled into TONE.md.

## 1. "Tone applies to every step, not just the intro." — GAP

**Evidence:**
- *5 P's* (multi-domain-lifestyle-psychoeducation-menu) — "doesn't read like a guide, it reads like a description ... ensure everything gets tone.md"
- *7 Cs of resilience* (multi-dimensional-framework-self-assessment) — "the entire activity, every step, needs tone.md"
- *tending your garden* (multi-domain-wheel-radar) — "apply tone.md, then repeat this process for each step of this activity"
- *my north star* (north-star-values-compass) — "the entire activity, every step, needs tone.md"
- *common myths about depression* (myth-busting-and-knowledge-quiz) — implicit
- *an act of kindness* (prosocial-acts-tracking) — "tone.md applied across it"

**Why it's a gap:** TONE.md `§Applying this` says "One pass per surface" — which authors read as "pick a surface and edit it." Nothing in the doc explicitly says **every step's middle copy gets the same care as the intro**. Authors are spending their tone budget on the intro and shipping mechanical bodies.

**Lands in:** `§Applying this` (top of section).

**Wording sketch:**
> **No coasting in the middle.** Tone is not the intro's job — it's the whole activity's job. If the intro lands and the middle steps read like a worksheet, the tone pass isn't done. Every prompt, every transition, every closing line gets the same revision energy the intro gets. The most common failure mode is a great intro followed by mechanical bodies; assume that's your activity until you've reread every step aloud.

## 2. "Read it as a guide, not a brochure." — GAP

**Evidence:**
- *5 P's* — "doesn't read like a guide, it reads like a description"
- *behavioral activation* (multi-step-ba-program) — "needs tone.md to its description"
- *common myths about depression* — "description reads very harshly"
- *substance use awareness* — "weak opener"
- *strengths card sort* (strengths-positive-qualities-inventory) — "after that, it reads like a description"
- *7 Cs of resilience* — "reads like instructions"

**Why it's a gap:** `§Intros` covers "make someone *want* to begin" and the three-beat narrative, but never explicitly contrasts **guide** vs **description**. The bar-test catches presence of a why; it doesn't catch "competent feature description with no human walking the user through."

**Lands in:** `§Intros` — added to the don'ts list after "Logistics are secondary."

**Wording sketch:**
> **Walk-with, don't describe-at.** An intro reads as if *you're standing next to them*, narrating what you're both about to do — never as if you're describing a product feature. Test: if you can lift the intro and drop it into a marketing page, it's a description. *"This activity will help you identify…"* is a brochure; *"So why don't we pull out just one and walk it through five steps…"* is a guide. The voice points forward into the work, not sideways at the work.

## 3. "Don't define the concept in the prompt — ask about the user's lived experience of it." — GAP

**Evidence:**
- *7 Cs of resilience* — "It's fine to start with 'Control', but don't define control, ask them a question"
- *substance use awareness* — "what does 'Did the wave carry you?' even mean?" (metaphor presented without question form)

**Why it's a gap:** `§Prompts` rules cover question-form, varied entry points, one-thing-per-question — but not the specific "for framework activities, the prompt names the C/P/etc. *as a question about their experience of it*, never as a definition of the concept."

**Lands in:** `§Prompts` — added to the rule list after "One thing per question."

**Wording sketch:**
> **For framework prompts (the C's, the P's, the corners, the rings): ask, don't define.** *"Where do you feel like things are out of your hands lately?"* — not *"Control is the sense of agency over your circumstances. Rate yours."* The framework is the intro's job; by the time the user is at the prompt, they're not here for definitions, they're here to put themselves on the page. The category label can sit above the question as a heading; the question itself is always a question.

## 4. "If the placeholder is doing the framing, the prompt is too thin." — BURIED

**Evidence:**
- *my management plan* (integrated-multi-component-treatment-plan) — "the prompts are too thin and don't follow our tone guide — a lot of what's in the placeholders can move to the prompts"
- *motivation and barriers* (motivation-barriers-analysis) — "apply tone.md to the placeholder text step 6"

**Why it's buried:** TONE.md line 160–162 already says "The prompt carries the question; the placeholder carries a concrete example." The inverse failure mode (placeholder carrying the *framing*) isn't called out, so authors mistake "I wrote a long placeholder" for "the prompt is sufficient."

**Lands in:** `§Prompts` — expand the existing prompt/placeholder line into a short paragraph.

**Wording sketch (replaces current line 160–162):**
> **The split, both directions.** The prompt carries the question and any framing the user needs to answer. The placeholder carries the example (`e.g. …`) — *only* the example. If you find your placeholder explaining what the question is *really* asking, or hinting at categories, or doing emotional reassurance — that work belongs in the prompt. Pull it back up; leave the placeholder as one clean modeled example. See [`EXAMPLES.md`](./EXAMPLES.md).

## 5. "Soften for who's actually reading." — GAP

**Evidence:**
- *relationship quality self-assessment* — "people with bpd might read this and soften the harshness that echoes to the person this is the target of"
- *roles we play in relationships* (interpersonal-dynamics-psychoed) — "super accusatory ... step 3 feels a little direct"
- *how I want to be treated* (personal-treatment-expectations-statement) — "way too direct"
- *common myths about depression* — "description reads very harshly"
- *self-care action planning* — "'pick the biggest one' feels pushy"

**Why it's a gap:** `§Shared voice` has "permission-granting, observational not prescriptive" — but doesn't name **who** the reader actually is. A user opening a relationship-quality assessment may have BPD, low self-esteem, or be in an abusive situation; the same phrasing reads differently to different people. No rule in TONE.md asks the author to imagine the reader.

**Lands in:** `§Shared voice` — added as its own bullet between "Warm is not flat" and "Human action."

**Wording sketch:**
> - **Picture the reader.** Someone opening a relationship-quality activity may have BPD; someone opening a depression-myths activity is already convinced something is wrong with them; someone opening a social re-engagement activity is isolated. The copy that reads "direct and helpful" to a well person reads "accusatory" to a struggling one. Soften imperatives, lead validation before challenge, and read every line as if the most fragile plausible user is opening it.

## 6. "Difficulty curve — each step is reachable from where the user stands." — GAP

**Evidence:**
- *social re-engagement ladder* (social-reengagement-activity-planning) — "'A real conversation with someone who knows you' feels absolutely devastating to someone who is socially isolated. we are trying to work people into it, not crash them"
- *opposite action* (emotion-override-opposite-action) — "step 2 feels like a rushed summary, not like walking someone through it"

**Why it's a gap:** `§Transitions` talks about format/mode shifts — never about emotional/behavioral difficulty. A "ladder" activity that puts the hardest rung in front of an isolated user has no rule guarding against that.

**Lands in:** `§Transitions` — added as a new sub-bullet.

**Wording sketch:**
> - **The difficulty curve is part of the pacing.** When an activity asks the user to do something gradient (a ladder, a graded exposure, a behavioral activation), each rung has to be reachable from where the user currently stands. The hardest rung in front of the most isolated reader is a crash prompt — they close the activity, not climb it. Start with the smallest plausible move (a wave, a one-word text, a step outside) and let the user choose how far up to go.

## 7. "Don't downplay heavy emotions — honor first, then redirect." — GAP

**Evidence:**
- *grief, or something more* (binary-screener) — "the tone here downplays grief when it should encourage them to work through grief but keep an eye out for signs that it's turning into depression"

**Why it's a gap:** TONE.md has "no empty reassurance" but no positive rule for **how to write about a heavy emotion** the user is actively in. Grief, suicidality-adjacent material, severe depression all need a different cadence than "let's pull out one thing and look at it."

**Lands in:** `§Shared voice` or new mini-section after it.

**Wording sketch:**
> **For heavy material — honor first, redirect second.** Grief, despair, suicidality-adjacent topics, severe shame: the copy's first job is to *honor the weight* (name that it's real, that it's heavy, that staying with it is the right thing for now). Only then does it move toward what the activity offers (notice patterns, watch for warning signs, plan ahead). Skipping the honoring beat to get to the redirect reads as "your feeling is in the way of my activity." That's the fastest way to lose a reader who is already running thin.

## 8. "Walk each named item in turn; don't bulk-summarize after the user has individuated." — GAP

**Evidence:**
- *mood-boosting sensory activity* — "the user chooses sensory activities, but i would like us to walk through each one that they chose"
- *treatment options overview* — "present each option in its own step with a response window for the individual's thoughts on it"

**Why it's a gap:** Not in TONE.md. When an earlier step has the user pick multiple distinct items, the natural next move is a per-item walkthrough — not a single closing summary that lumps them.

**Lands in:** `§Transitions`.

**Wording sketch:**
> - **If they named things, treat each thing.** When an earlier step has the user pick or name a list (three sensory activities, two treatment options, four roles), the activity should walk each one in turn — not collapse them into a single closing summary. The user did the work of individuating; honor that by giving each item its own moment.

## 9. "Carry-over is part of the read." — GAP (small)

**Evidence:**
- *focusing on positive feelings* (categorized-positive-reflection-worksheet) — "only show them the ones they picked. same with the carry over"
- *i-statement practice* (i-statement-framework-practice) — "compile the statement they completed in parts in step 2 at the top of step 3"
- *does this sound like me* (self-identification-checklist) — "explicitly carry over and display the things they selected"
- *my north star* — "ensure you're only carrying only the chips they selected in step 2 to step 3"
- *self-care action planning* — "explicitly displayed carryover"

**Why it's a gap:** `_SCHEMA.md` R5 covers the *mechanism* of carry-over. TONE.md doesn't cover its *reading experience*. A raw dump of every prior field is technically R5-compliant but reads as noise.

**Lands in:** `§Transitions` — added as a bullet, with a cross-ref to `_SCHEMA.md` R5.

**Wording sketch:**
> - **Carry-over is part of the read, not just a schema rule.** Show only what the user actually picked (not the unticked options), in their own words, with a plain-language label that names what they gave you ("the belief you brought" — not "belief"). A raw dump of every prior bind reads as noise; a curated mirror reads as care. R5 in [`_SCHEMA.md`](./activities/v2/_SCHEMA.md) is the mechanism; how it lands is the tone.

## 12. "Don't reference work-in-progress UI in body copy." — GAP

**Evidence:**
- *food diary* — "remove the 'coming soon' from step 4"
- *prepping for a stressful event* (event-based-coping-preparation) — "remove this: 'A small note: a monthly view here is on the way — your upcoming events laid out as soft-colored pills…'"

**Why it's a gap:** Not in TONE.md. The "no UI nouns" rule covers things like *button* / *slider*; it doesn't cover *apologetic future-tense pointers to unbuilt features*.

**Lands in:** `§Shared voice` — added to the "no UI" bullet.

**Wording sketch (expand the existing "Human action, never the UI" bullet):**
> - **Human action, never the UI — including the unbuilt UI.** "Keep track of how many times it slips in" — never "tap the counter." No interface nouns (counter, slider, field, button, screen, widget). And no future-tense apologies for features that don't exist yet ("a monthly view is on the way", "this is still being built"). The reader is here for what works *today*; gestures at WIP break the spell. Track them in `todo.md`, not in body copy.

## 13. "Closings honor the work; never a dump." — BURIED

**Evidence:**
- *core beliefs* (core-belief-work) — "feels like it should have some remediation step, it just kinda dumps you at the end"
- *medication history / adherence* — "use tone.md to write a nice closing"
- *my north star* — "i'd like a proper wrap up in step 5"

**Why it's buried:** `§Closings` exists but is the shortest section in TONE.md (3 bullets vs. ~10 for Intros). "Witness, don't report" implies honoring; it doesn't say "the closing names what they did and treats it as having mattered." Authors are technically following the section and still shipping dumps.

**Lands in:** `§Closings` — expand from 3 bullets to ~5; add the SummaryOutputCard closing line as part of the surface.

**Wording sketch (additions):**
> - **Honor what they did, by name.** A closing that ends an activity without acknowledging what the user just put on the page reads as a dump. Name the work: *"You looked at the belief from four angles"* / *"You named three small moves"* — then witness. The acknowledgement is the bridge between effort and meaning.
> - **The terminal SummaryOutputCard is part of this surface.** Its `closingPromptText` is the last thing the user reads. Treat it as a closing line, not as a system message: warm, specific to what they did, never a generic "Saved!" or "Activity complete."

## 14. "Side-door asides for paths that may not apply to everyone." — GAP (small)

**Evidence:**
- *social re-engagement ladder* — "like volunteering is always an option, right? i don't know if that's advice, but it could be an aside — like, even if you have no one to reach out to, there are places you can go to build this trust at the cost of only your time"

**Why it's a gap:** Not in TONE.md. Some activities assume the user has access (people to reach out to, money for treatment, a safe home). For users who don't, the default path is a dead end. A short aside opens a side door without making it the main route.

**Lands in:** `§Prompts` or `§Intros` — small new bullet.

**Wording sketch:**
> - **Offer a side door when the main path may not apply.** If an activity's default path assumes access the user may not have (people to reach out to, a partner to talk to, a doctor to call), include a brief aside naming an alternative the user can take alone (a volunteer setting, a written letter, a public space). Phrase it as an offer, not a fallback: *"And if there isn't a specific person — there's also…"*

## 16. "Imperative voice is OK in guided practice." — GAP (small exception)

**Evidence (Pass 2):**
- *body-scan* — "Begin at your feet. Slowly move your attention upward — calves, thighs, pelvis…"
- *breath-meditation* — "Follow the circle: expand on the inhale, contract on the exhale."

**Why it's a gap:** `§Shared voice` says "permission-granting, observational not prescriptive. No commands." That reads as a blanket ban. Guided meditations *must* use mild imperative ("breathe in", "begin", "follow") — and the two reference-shape non-beta meditations both do. Authors writing a new meditation may second-guess and water it down.

**Lands in:** `§Prompts` — a one-line exception note.

**Wording sketch:**
> - **Exception — guided practice.** Body-scans, breath meditations, paced sequences, and other guided practices use mild imperative voice as a feature, not a violation ("begin at your feet", "breathe in with the expansion"). The "no commands" rule applies to instruction-as-evaluation, not to the gentle leading voice of a guided practice. Keep imperatives short, soft, and paced; never stack three commands without a breath.
