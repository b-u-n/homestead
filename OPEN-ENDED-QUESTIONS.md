# Writing Open-Ended, Approachable Questions

These questions invite reflection without demanding it. They should feel like a hand extended, not a form to fill out.

---

## Core Rules

### 1. Vary the entry point
Don't start every question the same way. "Try to think of a recent time" is one way in — but you can also open with a feeling, a relationship, a pattern, an image, or a sensation, and arrive at the invitation from there. If the opener is identical each time, the questions blur together no matter how different the content is.

### 2. Let the escape hatch live anywhere
Every question should offer a way in for someone who doesn't have a ready example — but that doesn't have to be a second sentence appended at the end. It can be woven into the question itself, implied by open phrasing, or reframed so the "out" is part of the asking rather than a disclaimer after it.

### 3. One question does one thing
Resist the urge to define the emotional territory in advance — listing possibilities like "hurt, fear, exhaustion, feeling unheard" does the reader's work for them and narrows what they can bring. Ask the one thing and let them find their own word for what's underneath.

### 4. Match length to weight
A harder or more exposed question can afford to be shorter — the brevity itself creates space. A gentler question can breathe a little more. When every question is the same length, the emotional difference between them flattens out.

### 5. Let some questions end without a net
Not every question needs an explicit "or whatever you're thinking works too." Sometimes asking openly *is* the permission. Trust the reader to know their own answer counts.

---

## What to Watch For

- **Identical rhythm.** Read the questions aloud. If they all have the same cadence, restructure a few — fragment, invert, slow down.
- **Over-explanation.** If you find yourself glossing the question after asking it, the question probably isn't open enough yet. Revise the question instead.
- **The formula creeping back in.** The structure *[opener] + [scenario] + [escape hatch]* is a useful starting point, not a template. Break it when it's getting repetitive.

---

## Examples

**Is there someone you've been a little more careful around lately — choosing words, softening things, holding back a little? What's that been like?**

*Entry point: a relationship, not a moment. Escape hatch woven in — "a little more careful" is vague enough to include almost anyone.*

---

**Think of a moment you said something and immediately felt the relief of having said it. It doesn't have to be big. What made it possible that time?**

*Entry point: the feeling of relief first. "It doesn't have to be big" is the escape hatch, mid-question.*

---

**When did you last feel like you were waiting for something to be over — a conversation, a situation, a version of yourself? Whatever surfaces is fine.**

*Entry point: a feeling. Short. The escape hatch is the last clause, light and brief.*

---

**There's usually a gap between what we feel and what we say. Where does yours tend to live — do you know?**

*Entry point: a general observation, then a personal turn. "Do you know?" is the implicit permission to not know.*

---

**What's something you've been carrying around that you haven't found the right words for yet? You don't have to find them here either — just whatever comes.**

*Entry point: the carrying, not the thing. No escape hatch needed — the question already assumes imprecision.*
